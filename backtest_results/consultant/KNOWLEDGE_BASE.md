| Element | What it means in his framework | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|

- He calls himself technical-plus-fundamental. He will not act on a fundamental idea without confirmation from the chart. [S2] P, 2018 interview. Conf. H.

## 2. The VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern), as he describes it

### 2.1 What it is
- He describes the VCP as a supply/demand "footprint" that reflects the law of supply and demand. [S13] P, X post of 2021-09-01.
- Practitioners who study his material describe the VCP as a **characteristic of constructive bases**, not a separate geometric pattern. It can appear inside a cup-with-handle, a flat base, a double bottom or a 3C. [S22] 2, [S23] 2. Conf. M: this is consistent with his own charts, but I have no verbatim primary quote.
- The core idea is that volatility falls from left to right across the base, and volume dries up in specific places, especially at the right side and pivot. [S12] B, [S14] B. Conf. H.

### 2.2 Number of contractions ("T" count)
- A VCP usually has **2 to 6 contractions**. Book notes and summaries all give this range. [S14] B, [S12] B. Conf. H.
- Most bases show 2-4 contractions, and 5-6 are occasional. [S21] 2. Conf. M.
- He writes it as a **footprint** such as "40W 31/3 4T": 40 weeks long, a 31% widest correction, a 3% narrowest correction, and 4 contractions. [S14] B. Conf. H.
- How strict is the count? He presents it as descriptive, not as a pass/fail filter. His stated emphasis is on progressive tightening and volume behaviour. I found no accessible primary statement that a base with 7 contractions, or one with a single contraction, is automatically invalid. **Unknown** whether he ever states a hard rule. Conf. M.

### 2.3 Depth progression
- His rule of thumb is that each successive contraction is contained to **about half of the previous one, "plus or minus a reasonable amount"**. [S12] B, [S14] B. Conf. H. It is explicitly a rule of thumb, not a ratio test.
- He has been quoted with a typical first correction of about 20-33%, then 10-15%, then a final 3-8%. [S12] B, from a recap quoting him; the original venue is not identified. Conf. M.
- The "25% → 15% → 8% → 3%" sequence fits that description. **I could not find it verbatim in an accessible primary source**; secondary sources show 25/15/8 and 18/12/6. [S21] 2. Conf. L on the exact numbers.
- Overall depth:
  - constructive bases typically correct 10-35%, sometimes up to about 40% [S15] B;
  - very deep collapses of 60% or more are not SEPA setups [S24] 2. Conf. M.

### 2.4 Duration
- Commonly cited as **3 to 65 weeks**. [S21] 2, [S25] 2. Conf. M: I could not see it in a primary text.
- One set of book notes says typical bases last 5-26 weeks. [S14] B. Conf. M.
- The project spec's 15- to 325-trading-day bounds match the 3-65-week figure.

### 2.5 Volume
- Volume should contract as the base matures, most of all in the final tight area or pivot, where it is often well below the 50-day average. [S14] B, [S12] B. Conf. H.
- It should then expand on the breakout. [S12] B. Conf. H.
- The exact breakout-volume multiple is **unknown from primary sources**.
  - The "1.4× the 50-day average" figure appears only on vendor blogs [S26] 2. Conf. L.
  - The project's 1.4× (and the earlier 1.2×) should therefore be treated as our own calibration, not as his rule.
- **"Pivot leakage"** is his term (X, 2023-04-10). When many stocks set up, the next thing he checks is "the integrity of the bases and volatility contraction characteristics". The implication is that loose, leaky pivots lower quality. [S16] P. Conf. H that the term is his; conf. M on its exact definition.

### 2.6 The pivot, and the "cheat" entries
- **The pivot** is the top of the final, tightest contraction, the point where the stock leaves the line of least resistance. [S12] B, [S21] 2. Conf. H.
  - It is not necessarily the highest point of the whole base.
- **Buying ahead of the pivot:** he warns against buying before the pivot is breached in order to save a little. If the pivot is tight, getting in early gives little material advantage. This is a book passage quoted on X. [S17] B. Conf. H.
- **3C = "cup completion cheat"**:
  - an earlier entry off a pause (a mini-handle) that forms before the cup is complete;
  - in his words, the "cheat" area forms in the **lower to middle third of the base**, and a handle in the upper third is *not* a 3C (X, 2020-05-13). [S18] P. Conf. H.
- **Low cheat:** he uses the term himself, for example "'low cheat' pivot @ 322.16" (X, 2019-10-14). [S19] P. Conf. H.
  - A low cheat is an entry lower in the base than the classic cheat.
  - Book summaries describe it as taking a small early position with a tight stop, then adding as the stock clears the higher plateau. [S20] B. Conf. M.
- **Post-breakout behaviour:**
  - It is normal for a stock to clear the pivot and slip slightly back below it within 1-2 weeks. [S14] B. Conf. M.
  - A "squat" (a breakout that closes back in the range) is a warning, not an automatic failure. [S14] B. Conf. M.

### 2.7 What invalidates or weakens a VCP (as reported)
- Wide, loose price action, or heavy selling volume on the right side, meaning supply is still present near the pivot. [S24] 2, [S21] 2. Conf. M.
- Excessive depth: well beyond about 35-40% in a normal market, and 60% or more is not a setup. [S15] B, [S24] 2. Conf. M.
- Wrong stage:
  - a "VCP" that forms below the 200-day average is in Stage 1;
  - one that forms after a very extended late-stage run is in Stage 3. [S24] 2. Conf. M.
- Later bases are riskier than 1st and 2nd bases, and setups are more prone to fail late in a bull cycle. [S15] B. Conf. M.
- No progressive tightening. [S12] B. Conf. H.

### 2.8 How flexible the pattern is
- Every description frames the numbers as **typical ranges and rules of thumb**: "about half, plus or minus", "usually 2 to 6". [S12] B, [S14] B. Conf. H.
- He teaches the VCP by annotated charts and footprints, which is visual judgment. [S13] P, [S16] P, [S18] P.
- A practitioner reports that rigid swing-high rules misclassify real VCPs, for example ones with a slightly *higher* right-side swing high. [S23] 2. Conf. L-M.
- The one independent academic attempt at an algorithmic Minervini [S30] reports:
  - bases were "difficult to identify" algorithmically because of their subjective nature;
  - the author recommends using the algorithm to *exclude* stocks rather than to automate buying. Conf. H that the thesis says this.

## 3. Entry and exit practice

| Topic | What he says or does | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy at the pivot vs early | The default is the pivot breakout. Earlier buys come at cheat/low-cheat/3C pivots inside the base, with smaller size and a tighter stop. He warns against anticipating an untriggered pivot. | [S17] B, [S18] P, [S19] P, [S20] B | H |
| Max stop | "8 or 10 percent": if a trade cannot work within that, something is wrong. 10% is the ceiling. | [S2] P | H |
| Average loss | About 4-5% including slippage and earnings gaps (2018 interview). Secondary summaries give 5-6% or 6-7%. | [S2] P; [S27] 2 | H for 4-5% as his statement |
| Win rate | Right only about 50% of the time (2017 interview). "I'm wrong on my trades just as often as I'm right" (X, 2024-11-06). The edge comes from payoff asymmetry. | [S1] P, [S28] P | H |
| Payoff ratio | Keep average gain to average loss above 2:1, often 3:1. Don't let a good gain turn into a loss. | [S14] B, [S27] 2 | M |
| Selling | Selling is more subjective than buying. He sells into strength (into big advances) and into weakness (stops, violations). There is a whole chapter on selling in *Think & Trade Like a Champion*. | [S2] P, [S1] P, [S15] B | M on specific triggers |
| Progressive exposure | He scales up from small initial exposure (for example 25%, then 50% invested) only as trades work. "When my trades are working, I get very aggressive and I bet big" (X, 2024-11-08). He will re-enter a stock he sold, even at a higher price. | [S2] P, [S29] P | H |
| Risk per trade | 1.25-2.5% of equity per trade. | [S15] B | M |
| Concentration | 4-6 positions for small accounts and 10-12 for large, about 20 at most. Individual positions can be large (20-25% or more), justified by optimal-f/Kelly reasoning. | [S14] B, [S15] B; [S27] 2 | M (L on the exact 25%/50% caps) |
| Turnover | High: he is "not afraid of turnover". Another interview reportedly cites hundreds of trades a year with winners held about 3-4 weeks, but that figure appeared in page *comments*, so it is unverified. | [S29] P; [S1] (comment) | H / L |

## 4. The market-condition overlay
- He will hold **100% cash for months** when conditions are bad. [S2] P. Conf. H.
- His main gauge is bottom-up: the behaviour of his own breakouts and of leading stocks.
  - Pivot failures and degrading screen quality mean the list of buyable names has "contracted" (X, 2026-06-03). [S31] P.
  - In a "tricky" market where "a good number of breakouts have worked", the cost was still elevated (X, 2024-10-31). [S32] P.
  - Conf. H that this is his practice.
- Leaders tend to bottom before the general market, and new bull markets produce the biggest winners. [S15] B. Conf. M.
- Implication for us: the overlay is **feedback from recent trade outcomes and setup quality**, not only an index-trend rule. A backtest without it is testing a different method.

## 5. Fundamentals as he really uses them
- **Stated preference:** big and accelerating quarterly EPS growth, supported by sales and margins. [S3] P, [S14] B. Conf. H.
- **Flexibility:**
  - Requirements depend on the stock's **category**: Market Leaders, Top Competitors, Institutional Favorites, Turnarounds. [S3] P.
  - Big fundamental changes may not show in earnings yet; his example is a new drug approval. [S3] P. Conf. H.
- **Evidence from his disclosed trades:**
  - 12 of the 15 in-universe disclosed buys would fail our strict screen, for example OLLI, MATX and PSMT with EPS growth under 20%, and GWRE loss-making. [S40] (project, RESULT.md). Conf. H.
  - In practice, his fundamental "rules" act as preferences and ranking inputs, not vetoes.

## 6. Independent evidence on VCP / CAN SLIM performance and pitfalls

| Study / source | What it found | Caveats | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Torén 2023, Linköping MSc thesis [S30] | Algorithmic Minervini-style momentum on Swedish, Danish and Finnish stocks 2008-2023: excess returns in small caps, not in mid or large caps. Bases were hard to identify algorithmically, so the thesis recommends the algorithm as an *exclusion* tool. | No transaction costs; a "primitive variant" without the fundamental and catalyst elements; student thesis. | H (on what it says) |
| AAII CAN SLIM screens [S33][S34] | The original CAN SLIM screen shows +19.2% annualized price gain since 1998 vs +5.7% for the S&P 500 (July 2023); variants range from 12.2% to 15.4%. The screen often has 0 passing names. | Price-only, monthly rebalanced, equal-weighted; no commissions, spreads, slippage, dividends or taxes. | H (on numbers), L (as evidence of net edge) |
| CXO Advisory on AAII screens, 2018 [S35] | Screens beat benchmarks gross, but frictions cut the advantage; beating the indices required frictions under about 0.6%. Data snooping is plausible, and **outperformance deteriorated over time**. | An independent review. | M-H |
| Lutey, Hassan & Rayome 2018 [S36] | A modified, weighted CAN SLIM portfolio beat the DJIA risk-adjusted in 3 windows (weak evidence in the 16-year window). | Small journal; benchmark is the DJIA; details not re-verified. | L-M |
| Lo, Mamaysky & Wang 2000, *J. Finance* [S37] | Algorithmic chart-pattern detection (kernel regression) on US stocks 1962-96: some patterns carry **incremental information**. | Not a trading-profit test; the patterns were classic ones, not the VCP. | H |
| George & Hwang 2004, *J. Finance* [S38] | Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns and explains much of momentum, with no long-run reversal. | Supports the trend-template "near highs" component, not the VCP itself. | H |
| "VCP case study" of 36 setups averaging +69% [S39] | Hand-picked from the **top 100 performers** after the fact. | The author admits hindsight and survivorship bias, so this is **not evidence of edge**. | H (that it is biased) |
| Our own tests [S40][S41] | Exp 1 and 2 fail. In the fix tests, no arm beats MDY/IJR in both 2017-21 and 2022-26. | Survivorship (current members); design motivated by his 2021-26 picks. | H |

**Recurring pitfalls reported across these sources:**
1. **Hindsight selection:** pattern "studies" built from past winners, which includes his and O'Neil's own superperformance studies [S10]. Base rates among non-winners are unknown.
2. **Costs and slippage on breakouts.**
3. **Regime dependence:** VCPs cluster in bull phases [S39].
4. **Data snooping and decay after publication** [S35].
5. **Subjective base identification** that algorithms reproduce poorly [S30].

**Caveats:**
- **How the USIC verifies results:** by brokerage statements, sometimes with account walkthroughs. That is stronger than social-media claims, but it is **not a GIPS-audited composite** [S5] 2.
- **Self-selection:**
  - Entry is voluntary and costs a fee; monthly listing is optional [S5] 2.
  - Publicity goes to winners. A single year of one account cannot separate skill from luck.
- **One concentrated account:** 2021 was one account in one year, run with concentration and high turnover by his own description [S29].
  - Its size beyond "$1M+" is undisclosed.
  - Leverage use is **unknown**.
  - His published 2021 trade list was a *sample*, with no exits [S42].
- **The 2022 "record" press release was issued by his own company** [S4].
- **About the organiser:** in 1998 the contest organiser (Norman Zadeh) settled an SEC cease-and-desist order. It concerned unregistered hedge-fund offerings, and callers asking about the contests had been solicited to invest [S8].
  - This does not bear on Minervini's own results. It is context on how independent the organiser is.
- **Relevance to our backtests:** his record is evidence about *his discretionary trading*, including sizing, exits and the market overlay. It is not evidence that a mechanical selection rule derived from his books has an edge.

## 8. LLM judges and backtests (for the AI-chart-judge idea)
- **Look-ahead in LLMs:**
  - LLMs trained on data covering the backtest period can leak future knowledge.
  - Anonymizing company names reduced both look-ahead and a "distraction effect" in GPT-based return prediction.
  - The effect was stronger for large, well-known firms. [S43] (Glasserman & Lin 2023). Conf. H.
  - By analogy, *not tested*: famous charts such as NVDA 2023 may be recognisable even without a ticker.
- **Backtest overfitting:** the probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) framework, estimated with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, is the standard tool for estimating how likely a selected strategy is overfit. [S44] (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017). Conf. H.
