# Chapter 2 — "What You Need to Know First"
*Notes and analysis (not book text). Mostly mindset, with one recurring technical seed.*

## Core mindset takeaways
- **No paper trading** — author argues real money changes decision-making in ways simulation can't replicate. Relevant to us only as a human-trading opinion; irrelevant to Kashif since our whole system is virtual/paper by design (and that's staying, per our own standing principle).
- **Trading is a business, not a hobby** — treat it with the same rigor as running a company: consistent process, objective review of losses, no emotional attachment to being "right."
- **Cut losses fast, without ego** — the author's own turning point was mechanically selling losers early instead of holding out of pride. This is the qualitative root of what becomes a hard stop-loss rule later in the book.
- **Define one trading style and stick to it** — don't blend value/growth/day-trading/long-term approaches. For us this means Kashif's strategies should stay cleanly separated (which our multi-strategy, isolated-ledger design already does) rather than one blended "do everything" strategy.
- **Individual traders have structural advantages over big funds**: no committee approval, no forced diversification, ability to move fast and concentrate positions, ability to use hard stop-losses without moving the market. Argued advantage specifically applies to **small-float, less liquid stocks** — big funds can't easily trade them, individuals can. This is the first hint of a recurring theme: smaller share float = more room for a real price move. Worth carrying forward as a candidate screening factor.

## Relevance to Kashif
- No new hard numeric rule yet.
- **Watch for "float size" / shares outstanding** as a recurring factor — flagged here conceptually, likely quantified in a later chapter (fundamentals or SEPA chapter).
- Reinforces: strategies stay isolated, not blended (already in our design).
