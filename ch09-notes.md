# Chapter 9 — "Follow the Leaders"
*Notes and analysis (not book text). This chapter is about using individual leading stocks — not the index — to detect market turning points. Directly useful for the "market regime gate" idea we flagged back in Ch. 3.*

## Core idea
The strongest individual stocks turn up (and turn down) **before** the broad market index does. Rather than waiting for a top-down "the market has bottomed" signal, watch a basket of the best-behaving stocks — they're the real leading indicator.

## Concrete, mechanical signals for spotting a real bottom (usable as a market-regime gate)
1. **New-high list starts outpacing the new-low list** — the number of stocks hitting 52-week highs growing relative to those hitting 52-week lows is treated as real confirmation.
2. **"Lockout rally" pattern**: in a genuine new uptrend, pullbacks are shallow (roughly 3–5% peak-to-trough on the index) and don't come when "expected" — the market/leading stocks just keep grinding higher through overbought readings instead of correcting. Counterintuitively, **an index or stock ignoring an overbought signal is treated as a sign of strength, not a warning to wait for a dip.**
3. **Individual leading-stock behavior in a healthy advance**: roughly a 15–20% push, then a rest/pullback of only about 5–10% — shallow pullbacks relative to the preceding advance is itself a strength signal.
4. **Divergence**: leading stocks making higher lows while the general index is still making lower lows is treated as an early tell that a bottom is forming.

## A genuinely reusable quantitative rule: max drawdown ceiling before a new high
- **A healthy correction (peak to subsequent low) for a quality stock is roughly 25–35%**; up to ~50% during a severe bear market is tolerable.
- **A decline deeper than ~50% from peak is flagged as a real problem** — even if the stock later approaches a new high, the steep prior decline creates heavy "overhead supply" (a large pool of underwater holders likely to sell into any rally), making the eventual breakout more likely to fail.
- **Directly usable rule**: when evaluating a stock's base/consolidation before a potential Stage 2 entry (Ch. 5), check the depth of the prior decline from its last major high. Prefer ≤35%; treat >50% as a caution flag, not an automatic disqualifier, since it can still work but the failure rate is materially higher.

## Buying priority
- **Buy the strongest first** — among candidates that pass screening, prioritize by which ones break out earliest/strongest, not by personal preference or story. Let price action rank the shortlist.

## Sector/leadership rotation warning signal (downside version of the same idea)
- Just as leaders turn up first, **leaders topping and breaking down is an early warning for the sector/market**, often before the broad index shows real weakness. When index gains are increasingly carried by laggard stocks (rotation into weaker names) while former leaders stall, that's flagged as a late-cycle warning sign.
- **Leadership rarely repeats across cycles**: historically **fewer than 25% of one cycle's leading stocks lead the next cycle.** Design implication: don't bias screening toward previously-successful sectors/stocks — re-screen the full universe fresh each cycle rather than assuming past winners will keep winning.

## Sobering context on the downside of superperformance stocks (previews Ch. 12–13's urgency)
- **Roughly a third of superperformance stocks eventually give back all or more of their entire gain.**
- **Average subsequent decline from the peak: 50–70%.** In post-bubble conditions, some leaders fell 80–90%.
- This is the strongest argument in the book so far for *why* a firm exit/profit-taking discipline (Ch. 12–13) isn't optional — big winners are also capable of round-tripping completely without one.

## Relevance to Kashif
- **New market-regime signal candidates** (more mechanical/detectable than "is the index up or down"): ratio of new-52-week-highs to new-52-week-lows across the EGX universe; whether pullbacks in the leading-stock basket are shallow (3–10%) vs. deep.
- **New hard/soft-filter input**: max drawdown from prior high before the current base — favor ≤35%, flag >50% as higher-risk (don't hard-exclude, since the book notes it can still work, just less reliably).
- **Design principle, not a number**: don't hardcode a bias toward sectors/stocks that performed well historically on EGX — re-run screening on the full universe each cycle, since leadership rotates.
- Reinforces urgency of the risk-management chapters ahead: the same stocks capable of huge gains are also capable of giving all of it back without a firm exit rule.
