# Chapter 1 — "An Introduction Worth Reading"
*Notes and analysis (not book text). Mindset chapter — no numeric trading rules here.*

## Core mindset takeaways
- **Risk-first thinking**: the driving question before every trade should be "how much can I lose," not "how much can I gain." Reward is secondary to risk control.
- **"Conservative aggressive opportunist"**: aggressive in pursuing opportunity, but simultaneously risk-conscious. Not a contradiction — it's sizing/timing discipline layered on top of an aggressive search for winners.
- **Three decisions define trading**: what to buy, when to buy, when to sell. Everything in the book builds toward answering these three questions systematically, not by gut feel.
- **Discipline over talent**: the author frames his edge as acquired through years of deliberate study and error-correction, not innate skill. Relevant for us because it argues the *system* (rules) matters more than any individual's market intuition — which fits our "deterministic code decides" principle well.
- **Preparation vs. opportunity**: success comes from being fully prepared (rules, watchlist, criteria ready) before the opportunity appears — not reacting from scratch when a stock starts moving.

## Relevance to Kashif
Nothing here converts into a JSON rule. This chapter sets the philosophy the later rule-heavy chapters build on: risk-first, systematic, unemotional. Worth keeping as the "why" behind hard stop-losses and position sizing rules we'll define later (Chapters 12–13).
